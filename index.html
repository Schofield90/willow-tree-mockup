The problemWhy exercise stopped working somewhere in your forties

The classes got louder. Your recovery got slower. And the advice never changed: push harder, eat less. That advice was written for 25-year-olds. After 40, falling oestrogen takes muscle, bone density and sleep with it, and training built for someone else's body doesn't just stall. It puts you on the physio's table.

So most women quietly stop. Then the shopping gets heavier, the stairs get longer, and "I'm just getting older" starts to sound true.

It isn't true. You've been given the wrong training, not the wrong body.

The differenceWe're not selling workouts. We're keeping you capable.

Most fitness is sold on how you'll look. Ours is built on what you'll be able to do, this year and in thirty years:

  1. Get up off the floor without grabbing the furniture
  2. Carry the shopping in from the car in one trip
  3. Climb the stairs talking, not catching your breath
  4. Stay steady on an icy pavement
  5. Lift your case into the overhead locker yourself
  6. Spend an afternoon with the grandchildren and still have an evening
  7. Keep your independence as the years add up

Every class on our timetable exists to serve that list. Nothing here is about a transformation photo.

How it actually worksFour things you won't find in a standard ladies' fitness class

01

Built only for women over 40

Hormones, bone density, pelvic health, joints that have history. Most classes are designed for everyone and adapted for you if you ask. Ours are designed for you from the start, because you are the only person we coach.

02

A full movement menu, one membership

Conditioning, mobility, balance, Power Plate, yoga, Pilates, cardio, Restore & Release. Your body doesn't need the same thing every day, so you never have to choose whether you're "a Pilates person" or "a strength person". You're both, on different days.

03

Coached, not supervised

Classes are kept small enough that a coach notices your balance is weaker on one side, remembers your knee, and changes today's plan because of it. You are known by name here, not a number on a register.

04

Recovery counts as training

Some days we push. Some days we recover. Both are productive. A midlife body rewards intelligence, not punishment, so stretching, balance and Restore & Release sit on the timetable as equals, not afterthoughts.

A class tells you what to do for an hour. A coach helps you build a body for the next thirty years.

Said by every woman before she startedHonest answers to fair questions

"I'm too unfit to start."

You start where you are. That is the entire design. The coaches are women in their forties and fifties, and half the room began exactly where you are now.

"I don't know what I'm doing."

That's what coaching is for. Your first session starts with a conversation, not a fitness test, and nobody is left to guess at the back of the room.

"I can't jump around any more."

Then you won't. Mobility, balance, Pilates and Restore & Release sit on the same membership as conditioning. Your knees get a say in the plan.

"What if I miss a week?"

You come back the next one. The WhatsApp group will have noticed you were gone, and that's a feature, not a guilt trip.

"I work full time."

The first class of the day is at 6am and evening classes run at 5:30pm. The timetable is built around jobs and school runs, not around the studio.

"And after the eight weeks?"

You choose. Memberships run from £32 a month for four classes and roll month to month with one month's notice. Most women stay because it works, not because anything locks them in.
